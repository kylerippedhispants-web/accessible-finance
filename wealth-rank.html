What should I include?

Use what your household owns today, less what it owes today.

  • Include registered and non-registered investments, cash, property equity, pensions, vehicles, and business equity.
  • Subtract mortgages, loans, credit cards, and lines of credit.
  • Do not include annual income in these net worth fields or use the full value of a home without also subtracting its mortgage.

Use the number well

A rank is context, not a verdict.

01

Compare like with like

Age, home ownership, family size, pensions, and regional housing costs can move net worth dramatically. Start with your age-peer view before drawing conclusions.

02

Watch the direction

A rising net worth over several years is usually more useful than a single percentile. Track assets and debts consistently, using the same definitions each time.

03

Focus on controllables

Debt reduction, an emergency reserve, regular investing, and avoiding concentrated bets matter more than chasing somebody else's rank.

Data, method, and important limitations

The national wealth curve interpolates between published family net worth benchmarks. Age and province estimates scale that curve using corresponding official medians. Income views use official 2023 wage medians by age, sex, and province.

Single-age lines, percentile ranges, compositions, and histograms are transparent estimates between published anchors. Statistics Canada reports wealth for family units and wages for individual tax filers, so the page keeps those units labelled separately.

  • Results are estimates rounded for readability.
  • Negative net worth is supported.
  • Territories are excluded because the cited SFS provincial table covers the 10 provinces.
  • The future scenario uses today's dollars and does not model tax, fees, or volatility.

Sources: Statistics Canada Survey of Financial Security, 2023; SFS assets and debts by age; 2023 wages by age and sex; 2023 wages by province and sex; Parliamentary Budget Officer high-net-worth families update.
